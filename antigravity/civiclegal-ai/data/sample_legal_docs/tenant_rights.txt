FAIR HOUSING ACT (42 U.S.C. § 3604) — OVERVIEW
=============================================================

The Fair Housing Act (FHA) prohibits discrimination in the sale, rental, and financing of housing based on:
- Race
- Color
- National origin
- Religion
- Sex
- Familial status (presence of children under 18)
- Disability

KEY TENANT RIGHTS UNDER FEDERAL LAW:

1. RIGHT TO NOTICE BEFORE EVICTION
   Under most state laws (which supplement federal minimums), landlords must provide:
   - 30 days notice for month-to-month tenancies
   - 60 days notice in many states for long-term tenants
   - Written notice specifying the reason for eviction

2. RIGHT TO HABITABLE CONDITIONS
   The implied warranty of habitability (recognized in nearly all states) requires landlords to:
   - Maintain structural integrity of the building
   - Provide heat, water, and electricity
   - Keep common areas safe and clean
   - Make necessary repairs within a reasonable time

3. SECURITY DEPOSIT PROTECTIONS
   Most states require landlords to:
   - Return security deposits within 14–30 days of move-out
   - Provide itemized written documentation of any deductions
   - Keep deposits in separate escrow accounts in some jurisdictions

4. PROTECTION FROM RETALIATION
   Under 42 U.S.C. § 3617, landlords may not retaliate against tenants for:
   - Reporting housing code violations
   - Organizing tenant associations
   - Filing complaints with HUD or local housing authorities

5. ADA REASONABLE MODIFICATIONS
   Under the Fair Housing Amendments Act (FHAA):
   - Tenants with disabilities may make reasonable modifications at their own expense
   - Landlords must allow these modifications
   - Tenants may be required to restore the unit at move-out
